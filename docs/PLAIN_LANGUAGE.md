# Explain a research connection

Use four short parts:

1. What happened: a dated observation or clearly attributed claim.
2. Why it matters: people, ecosystems or systems plausibly exposed.
3. How effects might spread: conditional steps, with evidence and alternatives for each.
4. What to watch: measurable indicators, timing and a rule that could challenge the hypothesis.

Label uncertainty where the reader encounters it. An empty map is not evidence of an uneventful world. A source count is not a credibility score. Explain date types and geographic precision. Original forecasts remain visible even when later evidence disagrees.

All examples in the local demo are fictional. Any real research dataset would need a separate review and publication decision.
