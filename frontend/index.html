HOW TO READ THE ATLAS

Connections need evidence.

We read global and regional media across disciplines, develop original conditional mechanisms, and test key assumptions against primary data. Reprints of one wire report remain one source family.

01 / Observation

What was measured, where and when. Publication time is not the observation window.

02 / Hypothesis

Why one change might transmit into another. Every edge needs its own evidence and conditions.

03 / Forecast

An expectation recorded before the outcome. Original wording, horizon and probability stay unchanged.

04 / Evaluation

Support, counterevidence or unresolved. Missing data are neither confirmation nor refutation.

Assess significance through scale, duration, affected people and sectors, systemic connections and evidence strength. Repeated small incidents can form an important pattern. Media attention is not severity.

Work limit: 3 actively tested hypotheses. The mechanism library may be larger. Uncertainty grows through multi-step chains. Calibration means comparing forecasts with outcomes and improving the research process, not retraining the model.

Events need two independently originating sources for the exact claim and period. Authoritative measurements and official decisions may instead use one primary origin with an independent authenticity, context and interpretation check. Context is not a second measurement. Historical observations and unverified candidates remain clearly distinguished.

Conflict analysis stays at a public, high level. No live tactical information. We store source notes and links, not full articles.
